# Card sequence

A carousel is read at the reader's own pace, so the order does the persuading.

| Card | Role (`role`) | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `ouverture` | The cover: the question or the hook, eight words or fewer. |
| 2 | `contenu` | The answer or the orientation. Useful on its own. |
| 3 to n-2 | `contenu` | One thing per card: an explanation, an attestation, a step of the path. Each carries its source. |
| n-1 | `contenu` | What is established, what is proposed, what stays open. |
| n | `bascule` | The closing: connect to the project and invite the reader to contribute. Carries no source. |

- One card says one thing.
- A comparison puts the compared cases on the same criteria, card after card.
- A timeline card needs two to four dates that the sources actually support.
- A map card needs a place or a route that is documented.
- Pairs (`paires`): a two-to-four term parallel, each term with a gloss, when the
  equivalence itself is the content; the body says where it comes from.
- Read the deck at phone width first: if a card needs more than a few lines, it is
  two cards.
