# Zeigarnik effect — the open loop

**Status:** observed
**Filed:** 2026-09-07
**Source:** LinkedIn post by Youna Bentabed ("Je décode le hacking humain |
Cyber intel…"), published c. 2026-08-31, brought in by the operator on
2026-09-07. A practitioner asserting a principle from the social-engineering
side — not a study, and not measurement of our audience. The underlying
finding is Bluma Zeigarnik's 1927 work, from Kurt Lewin's group, on the
retention of interrupted versus completed tasks. **Later replications of the
original laboratory effect have been mixed**; treat the psychological
literature as context, and our own numbers as the evidence. Do not cite the
1927 study publicly without verifying it first-hand.

## What it is

The mind does not tolerate an unfinished story. An incomplete narrative creates
a cognitive tension, and that tension pushes toward closing the gap. In the
source's own compression:

> Curiosité → inconfort → action. C'est automatique.

The viewer does not stay because the subject is interesting. They stay because
leaving costs them something.

## How it applies to a 30–45 s vertical

It is not one technique among others — it is the load-bearing structure of the
whole format. See `references/doctrine.md`:

- **Beat 1** opens the debt. A flat impossible assertion works better than a
  question because the objection is raised by the viewer, not handed to them.
- **Beats 2–4** keep it open by making the resolution feel worth waiting for.
- **Beat 5**, the reframe, closes it.
- **The exit re-open** deliberately opens a small second loop after the first
  is settled, and leaves it dangling. Costs three seconds, buys the rewatch.

The practical writing test: after the first sentence, can the viewer say what
the video will conclude? If yes, there is no loop, and no amount of production
quality will put one back.

## The fork

The source states it plainly, and it is the reason this pattern is filed with a
fork section at all:

> En communication, on s'en sert pour captiver, en social engineering, on s'en
> sert pour manipuler.
> "Votre compte sera suspendu… cliquez ici pour en savoir plus."

Identical device, opposite intent. The phishing message and the hook above both
open a loop the target cannot leave alone. What separates them is **whether the
debt is real and whether it gets paid.**

Our three rules:

1. **The loop must be closed** — in the video, or by a click that genuinely
   answers the question the hook raised.
2. **The debt must be real.** A loop opened on an invented fear, an invented
   stake, or a figure the source cannot state is manipulation whatever the
   intent behind it.
3. **The closing line answers the hook's own question**, in the hook's own
   terms. Changing the subject at the CTA is an unpaid debt wearing a
   resolution's clothes.

On a sourced, decolonial atlas there is a fourth: a loop opened on the viewer's
identity must hand something back. The Bantu script takes away a label and
returns *chaque peuple a pourtant son propre nom.* Without that return it is not
a hook, it is a provocation.

## Evidence in our own record

EthniAfrica's YouTube channel, 16 subscribers, September 2026 — the widest gap
in the record, and it is a gap in structure, not in production quality:

| Video | Loop opened? | Views |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nigeria | yes, + exit re-open | **861** |
| Afrique | yes (question form) | 706 |
| Lingala | yes | 632 |
| Channel presentation | **no** | **13** |

The presentation video was not worse made. It was not owed.

Within the loops, the shape matters too: the flat assertion (Nigeria, 861) beat
the direct question (Afrique, 706) on the same channel in the same week. One
week, one channel, three data points — a direction, not a law.
