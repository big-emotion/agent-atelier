# What a scripting session delivers

The deliverable is **an approved script and everything needed to defend it** —
not a video, and not a paragraph of advice. If the session ends without the
items below, it has not finished.

## The seven deliverables

1. **The narration, verbatim, in French.** Beat by beat, exactly as it will be
   spoken. This is the thing the operator approves, and once approved it is
   frozen: no sentence is deleted, softened or accelerated downstream to fit a
   duration.
2. **The mechanism note.** One line per beat: which device, and why this subject
   needs it. This is what teaches while it serves — the operator asked to learn
   the field, not to be handed finished copy.
3. **The claim map.** Every factual sentence in the narration → its anchor in
   the project's corpus or source, with the tier that source carries. A claim
   with no anchor does not enter the script; it becomes a question for the
   editorial owner.
4. **The rhetoric flags.** Which sentences are editorial emphasis rather than
   sourced findings, marked as such **in the deliverable itself**. This is not
   optional. The strongest sentence in the best-loved EthniAfrica script is
   rhetoric, and it is only safe because the memory says so.
5. **The speech/text split.** For every foreign, historical or homographic form:
   spoken, or shown as a text card — with the reason. The voice collapses
   homographs and has already destroyed an etymological argument silently.
6. **The pronunciation flags.** The proper nouns a human must listen to before
   publication. Neither the agent nor the alignment report can hear.
7. **The closing line**, with the subject class that justifies it (an ethnonym,
   a place name, or neither).

On request, add **per-surface hook variants** — the hook rewritten for TikTok,
Instagram, Facebook and a LinkedIn text post. The hook is rewritten for the
surface, never copy-pasted across it. Which surfaces actually get the piece is
the content strategist's call, not this skill's.

## The gate before hand-off

Walk it in order. The first failure stops the hand-off.

- [ ] The hook is **spoken**, and the viewer could not have predicted it.
- [ ] There is **exactly one reframe sentence**, and it is at beat 5.
- [ ] Every claim has an anchor and a tier; rhetoric is flagged as rhetoric.
- [ ] Nothing is promised that the destination cannot answer.
- [ ] No figure was invented to land a beat — where the source declares a
      quantity unknowable, the script says so or stays silent.
- [ ] No foreign form is spoken that the voice would collapse into another.
- [ ] Every diacritic in an on-screen card is one the render can be trusted to
      produce; if not, a safe form was chosen.
- [ ] The closing line answers the hook's own question and matches the subject
      class.
- [ ] The script's length is whatever the argument needs. No sentence was cut
      to match a previous export.
- [ ] The subject is not being mocked. Where the material invites mockery, the
      script lands on the claim instead.

## What needs the operator's approval before anything downstream

- **The final French narration**, per video, before any audio is generated.
- **Any deviation from the project's approved production format.**
- **Publication.** Nothing is posted from a scripting session.

## Hand-off

The approved script goes to the project's production reference. On EthniAfrica
that format is written and frozen — voice, pacing, canvas, captions, brand
ending, deliverables. **Do not redesign it from a scripting session**, and do
not restate it here; read it where it lives.
