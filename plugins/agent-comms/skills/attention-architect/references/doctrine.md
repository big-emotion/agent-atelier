# Attention doctrine — short-form video and social copy

This doctrine is **derived from measured productions, not from a marketing
manual.** Every rule below points at a script that shipped and a number it
produced. Where a rule rests on outside literature instead, it is filed in
`patterns/` and cited as such.

Source material: three EthniAfrica production memories and the eight approved or
drafted narrations they contain, written between 2026-09-04 and 2026-09-07. The
memories themselves stay in that project's own production directory; what
transfers is recorded here.

---

## 1. The loop is the whole product

A short video does not hold attention by being interesting. It holds attention
by **owing the viewer something**. The opening sentence creates a debt; the
viewer stays because an unpaid debt is uncomfortable; the video ends by paying
it. That discomfort is the Zeigarnik effect — see
`patterns/zeigarnik-open-loop.md` for the mechanism and its ethical fork.

Two consequences that decide most rewrites:

- **A video with no debt has no retention floor.** The EthniAfrica channel
  presentation opened no loop and took **13 views**, against 632–861 for the
  three shorts built on this shape, on the same channel in the same period.
  That is the widest measured gap in the record and it is not about production
  quality — the presentation video was not worse made, it was not owed.
- **A debt that goes unpaid converts once and never again.** The click is not
  the goal; the closed loop is. See `SKILL.md`, "The line this skill does not
  cross".

---

## 2. Beat 1 — the hook

**It is spoken.** A silent title card followed by unrelated narration was
explicitly rejected on this format. The first thing the viewer hears is the
debt itself.

Four hook shapes are in the measured record. Ranked by what they produced:

### a. The flat impossible assertion — the strongest

A short declarative sentence stating something that sounds like it cannot be
true. No question mark, no build-up, no "saviez-vous que".

> Le pays le plus peuplé d'Afrique porte un nom trouvé par une journaliste, pour éviter une phrase trop longue.

**861 views** — the best of the three published. The loop is not a question the
viewer is asked, it is an objection they raise themselves: *that can't be right,
prove it.* A self-raised objection is a stronger debt than an asked question,
because the viewer owns it.

Same shape, drafted: *"Le Cameroun porte le nom d'une crevette."* (six words),
*"Le Ghana porte le nom d'un empire qui n'a jamais été sur son territoire."*,
*"Le Sénégal s'appelle peut-être « notre pirogue »."*

**Write it as short as the claim survives.** The Cameroun hook is six words and
loses nothing.

### b. The withheld identity

State the fact, withhold the name.

> Pendant quatre-vingt-dix ans, ce pays a porté le nom d'un homme.

Two unknowns at once — which country, which man — and the viewer cannot resolve
either without staying. Use when the subject's name is itself the reveal. Do
not use twice in a series: it reads as a tic.

### c. The direct question

> Afrique. Cinquante-quatre pays, trente millions de kilomètres carrés. Vous savez d'où vient ce nom ?

**706 views.** It works, and it works less well than (a) on the same channel in
the same week. A question invites the viewer to answer "yes" and leave. Prefer
it when the subject word is one the viewer uses daily without ever having
wondered — the scale statement before the question is what makes the ignorance
feel worth fixing.

### d. The identity challenge

> Fier d'être Bantu ? Mais « Bantu », ce n'est pas le nom d'une ethnie ou d'un peuple unique. C'est une étiquette créée par un linguiste européen.

The strongest possible debt — it is the viewer's own self-description being
contradicted — and the most dangerous. It only survives if the video hands
something back. This one does: *chaque peuple a pourtant son propre nom.* A
video that takes an identity away and returns nothing earns a hostile comment
section, not a click.

**Never use (d) on a subject the corpus cannot resolve.**

### What is not a hook

A logo. A title card. A greeting. A statement of what the channel is. Anything
the viewer could have predicted from the thumbnail.

## 3. Beats 2–4 — sustaining the debt

The middle is not filler and it is not "the explanation". It is the sequence of
things the viewer needs in order to keep believing the hook is worth resolving:

- **Plausibility** — the setup that stops them calling it a lie and swiping.
- **Proof** — a primary document, a date, a name, a figure. Dated and specific:
  *"Le 8 janvier 1897, Flora Shaw publie un article dans le Times."* A vague
  proof beat re-opens the objection instead of settling it.
- **Context** — what surrounds the fact and lets the reframe land.

**Specificity is a retention device, not pedantry.** *164 voix pour, une seule
contre.* *Trente millions de kilomètres carrés.* *Du troisième au treizième
siècle.* Round approximations read as invention and cost the belief the hook
just bought.

---

## 4. Beat 5 — the reframe sentence

**Every approved script has exactly one, in the same position, and it is the
sentence people quote.** It restates the opening absurdity as a meaning. Writing
it is the single highest-leverage act in the whole script.

| Video | Reframe |
| --- | --- |
| Bantu | Le mot d'un linguiste a effacé des centaines d'identités. |
| Afrique | Un peuple d'une province a fini par nommer trente millions de kilomètres carrés et cinquante-quatre États. |
| Cameroun | Le nom donné à un fleuve, pour ce qu'on y pêchait, a fini par désigner tout un pays. |
| Ghana | Ce n'est donc pas une erreur de géographie. C'est une revendication. |
| Zimbabwe | Le pays a échangé le nom d'un colon contre celui de ses propres bâtisseurs. |
| Corriger la carte | Le problème n'est pas qu'une carte de marins déforme. C'est qu'on l'ait enseignée comme la carte du monde. |

Two constructions carry all six: **the disproportion** (a small cause named an
enormous thing) and **the correction** (*ce n'est pas X, c'est Y*).

**The reframe is where rhetoric sneaks in.** The Bantu line is the most forceful
in the record and the production memory flags it explicitly as editorial
emphasis, not an established historical finding. Write the reframe as hard as
the subject allows, then check it against the source before approval — and never
carry a previous script's rhetoric forward as if it were a fact.

---

## 5. The exit re-open

The best-performing script does not end on its reframe. It ends on a new,
smaller loop, opened at the exit and deliberately left dangling:

> En 1914, Frederick Lugard officialise ce nom lors de la fusion des protectorats.
>
> **Cet homme, elle l'épousera en 1902.**

Nigeria, **861 views** — the only script of the three carrying this device, and
the only one to break 800. The main debt is paid first; the extra one costs
three seconds and buys the rewatch and the comment.

Conditions: the re-open must be **true, sourced, and secondary**. It cannot be
the thing the video owed. Do not force one where the material has none — an
invented kicker is exactly the bait this doctrine refuses.

---

## 6. Declared doubt is a device

> Puis le fleuve a donné son nom au pays. Peut-être. On ne le saura jamais tout à fait, et c'est écrit ainsi dans l'atlas.

Uncertainty does not weaken the video, it authenticates it — and on a sourced
atlas the doubt *is* the product. Say "probablement" out loud when the fiche
says probablement, and make the admission the proof of the posture.

This only works if it is true. A manufactured hesitation is a manipulation.

---

## 7. Beat 6 — paying the debt

The closing line answers the question the hook raised. **It never changes the
subject**, and it never generalises past what the episode was about.

The measured rule on EthniAfrica: the closing line matches the **class of the
subject**.

- an ethnonym, a people's own name → *Retrouve-les sur EthniAfrica* / *chaque peuple*
- a country or place name → *Retrouve l'histoire de chaque pays sur EthniAfrica*
- neither → write the line the episode actually earned (*Retrouve les vraies
  proportions de l'Afrique sur EthniAfrica*)

A closing line promising "chaque peuple" on an episode about a toponym is a
small unpaid debt, and it accumulates across a series.

## 8. Constraints from production that bind the writing

These are not production details — they change the words, so they belong to
scripting. Full list in the project's production memory; these are the ones that
have already destroyed an argument.

- **The voice collapses homographs.** A script contrasting spoken *Africa*,
  *Ifrīqiya* and *Afrique* was rendered as three identical words, silently
  erasing the etymology. **Foreign and historical forms appear as text cards,
  not as speech** — decide deliberately, per form, and write the decision into
  the script. Affects *Rio dos Camarões*, *dzimba-dza-mabwe*, *sunu gaal*.
- **Diacritics may not render.** A macron was dropped rather than risk an
  unrendered glyph. Prefer a form that survives.
- **Neither the agent nor the alignment report can hear.** Proper nouns need a
  human listen-through before publication — *Wagadu*, *Nkrumah*, *sunu gaal*,
  *Rio dos Camarões*, *Mercator*. Flag them in the script.
- **The script sets the length, never the reverse.** The reference short ran
  32.7 s of narration in a 34.8 s video; that is an observation, not a budget.
  Never delete a sentence or accelerate speech to hit a previous export's
  duration.

## 9. The loop travels differently per channel

The device is universal; the vehicle is not.

**Measured, EthniAfrica:** on LinkedIn, three video posts produced **zero
clicks between them**, while two text posts produced **all** of the clicks. On
that channel the loop is opened in the first line of text, above the fold, and
the video is not the vehicle at all.

So: same subject, same debt, different opening surface. TikTok gets the short
interrogative caption; Instagram gets the longer sourced detail; Facebook gets
the link inside the post; LinkedIn gets a text post whose first line carries the
hook. Distribution decisions themselves belong to the project's content
strategist — this doctrine only says that the hook must be re-written for the
surface, never copy-pasted across it.

---

## 10. Critique mode — the leak audit

When judging an existing script or a video that underperformed, walk the beats
in order and name the first one that fails. Attention leaks forward: a broken
beat 1 makes beats 2–6 unmeasurable.

1. Is there a debt in the first sentence, and is it **spoken**?
2. Could the viewer have predicted that sentence from the thumbnail?
3. Does beat 2 stop the objection, or restate the hook?
4. Is the proof dated, named and specific?
5. **Is there a reframe sentence at all?** Its absence is the most common defect
   in a script that "explains well" and still flattens.
6. Does the closing line answer the hook's own question, in the hook's own terms?
7. Is anything promised that the destination cannot deliver?

Report the first failure and the rewrite. Do not list all seven when the first
one decides the outcome.
