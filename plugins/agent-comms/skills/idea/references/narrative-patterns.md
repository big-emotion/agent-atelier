# Narrative pattern catalogue

Ten patterns are examined for every subject. There is no quota of proposals: a
pattern the evidence cannot carry is reported with its reason (`unsupported`,
`inapplicable` or `conditional`). A proposal chooses one main pattern; a secondary
device, such as an anecdotal opening, is described in its arrangement and never
combined into an eleventh pattern. The pattern says how the argument unfolds; the
visual profile of the deck is a separate choice.

A proposal's success criterion is a learning goal bounded by the evidence ("the
sources establish X; they do not establish Y"), never an audience measure: nothing
here claims an audience was tested.

## Patterns

| Id | Question it answers | Ordered moves | Reader can afterwards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `origin-explained` | Where does this come from? | form and meaning at the source; the context it arose in; how it reached today | state the explanation, its context and how certain it is |
| `competing-explanations` | Why are there several readings? | put the same questions to each reading; compare what each really explains; say what the evidence settles and what stays open | tell the readings apart without crowning one the evidence does not support |
| `chronological-trajectory` | What changed over time? | first attested state; the documented turning points; the current state and what links them | order the documented changes and tell a first attestation from a creation |
| `perspectives` | Who sees this how? | who uses the term or holds the view; who it concerns and in what context; what each relation says and does not say | attach each usage to its documented context without making a label anyone's permanent identity |
| `circulation` | How did it travel? | the documented starting point; the attested carriers, places and channels; the links that are only inferred, said as such | tell attested stages from inferred links |
| `turning-point` | What changed at that moment? | the situation before; the event or action; the consequences; the bounded reach of the change | explain a documented before and after and the limits of the event |
| `key-actor` | What role did this person or body play? | the starting situation; the documented action; the effects; the contribution weighed against others' | name the supported contribution without turning codifying or spreading into inventing |
| `clarifying-comparison` | What differs between these cases? | define each case from its own source; compare on the same explicit criteria; say how they relate and where the comparison stops | state a supported similarity or difference without ranking |
| `anecdote-entry` | What does this concrete scene reveal? | the scene; the question it raises; the wider explanation; back to the scene | explain the mechanism the scene illustrates and what one anecdote cannot establish |
| `received-claim-examined` | Is this commonly held idea accurate? | define the idea's terms; the evidence; the qualification or correction; the precise answer | restate the idea and the evidence-backed answer without swapping it for another unsupported claim |

## The six common functions

Every proposal accounts for B1 to B6. They may be merged, repeated or interleaved;
they are not six mandatory cards. B1 opens, B5 answers before B6 closes. Only B2
to B4 may be omitted, with a reason.

| Id | Block | Function | Question to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Pose the question | State a precise promise and locate the subject | What exactly is being discussed? |
| B2 | Give the core explanation | The central answer or answers proposed | What is the explanation, and who holds it? |
| B3 | Anchor in traces and actors | Attestations and actors behind the explanation | Since when is it attested, and who uses, transmits or formalises it? |
| B4 | Tell the path | The movement towards the present | How did it spread or change? |
| B5 | Answer clearly | What the reader can keep | What is established, what is proposed, what is still unknown? |
| B6 | Close | Connect to the project and invite contribution | Which source or account could complete the story? |

## Reading them as a carousel

A reader sets their own pace, goes back, compares cards, and may share one card
without its neighbours. So: a useful answer or orientation arrives by card 2; the
full payoff never depends on a second carousel; a qualified claim carries its
qualification on the card, not only in the caption; six functions are not six
cards (one card may carry several, one function may take several cards).
